I started college studying law, where I became interested in how people interpret systems, make decisions, and navigate complexity. When I later discovered UX design, it felt like an extension of those same interests, applied in a more hands-on and creative way.

                
That curiosity eventually led me to study computer science👩‍💻💻🤯🫩⌨️🧠🫠 as well, because I wanted to understand not just the people I was designing for, but also the technical systems behind the products themselves.

                
Having both design and technical foundations has shaped the way I work today. I enjoy translating complex systems into experiences that feel clear, useful, and approachable for people who may not have technical backgrounds. I tend to think beyond individual screens or flows and focus on the bigger picture: the product strategy, the operational constraints, and how decisions impact users across the entire experience.

                
That mindset led me to work as a product manager at an early-stage startup, and later to design enterprise AI and data platforms at Intuit, where I’ve led multiple products as the sole designer01/01 in close collaboration with cross-functional teams. I’m especially excited by complex domains like AI, developer tools, and enterprise systems, where thoughtful UX can make a meaningful difference in how people work.

                
Outside of work, I love ✈traveling to new cities and learning through their history, culture, architecture, and art. I usually end up spending hours in local museums, walking through neighborhoods, and finding the places locals actually love. I’m also a huge baseball fan, so I’m always happy to talk about great stadiums, travel recommendations, or your favorite team.
